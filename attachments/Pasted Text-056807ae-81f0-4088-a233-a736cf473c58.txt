affordances        Affordances are the qualities or properties of an object that show a user how it can be used and interacted with.Core DefinitionAction possibilities: An affordance is a link between an object and a person.Relational: It depends on both the object and the user's physical or mental abilities.No instructions needed: Good affordances let people know what to do just by looking. For example, a physical button invites pushing, and a mug handle invites gripping.Origins in Psychology and DesignJames J. Gibson: An American psychologist who first coined the term in 1977. He defined affordances in ecological psychology as what the real environment offers to an animal or human.Don Norman: A design expert who brought the idea into Human-Computer Interaction (HCI) and UX design through his book The Design of Everyday Things. He focused heavily on what users perceive they can do with an interface or object.Types of AffordancesPerceptible: Clear and visible properties that correctly show how an object works (like a protruding door handle).Hidden: Properties or functions that exist but are not obvious or easy to see right away.False: An object looks like it can be used a certain way, but it actually cannot (like a broken touch screen button).If you want, let me know:Are you studying affordances for UX/UI design?Or are you looking at them from a psychological perspective?I can give you more targeted examples or details.YouTube·Love & Philosophy with Andrea Hiott15mWhy everyone is talking about 'affordances' with Harry Heft and ...IxDFWhat are Affordances? — updated 2026 | IxDFWhat are Affordances?
